From matrices to PEs, wavelets and colours

Before following a run, here is how MeshGEMM's inputs are cut up and carried. The same rules apply at every size; the traced run uses 64×64 matrices on a 4×4 mesh, so every PE holds 16×16 tiles. The toy at the end uses the same mesh and the same schedule with 1×1 tiles, small enough to check by hand.

3 · Which colour carries what

A colour is a 5-bit tag on every wavelet, from 24 IDs (0–23). It is not a separate wire: all colours share each physical link, and each router holds one route per colour (rx port → tx port) plus a buffer per colour. MeshGEMM's layout binds 12 IDs: X on 1–3, W on 4–6, and the X skew on 7–9 (backward) and 12, 13, 16 (forward). The memcpy framework reserves 21–23. The remaining IDs (10, 11, 14, 15, 17–20) are unused.

5 · Toy: a 4×4 GEMM you can check by hand

X and W are 4×4 integer matrices, and each PE holds a single number of each (1×1 tiles). The schedule is the real one: the k-block each PE multiplies at each step is the one verified in the trace. Step through it, then tap a PE to see its four terms add up to its entry of C.
