Inside one processing element

Every GEMM on this machine comes down to one instruction, and the only question a dataflow answers is where each of its operands comes from. The FMA computes dest = src0 + src1 × src2: src0 and dest are the accumulator, a vector in memory; src1 is a second vector streamed from memory; and src2 is a single scalar broadcast to every lane. The three schemes fill those three slots differently.

    On the inner-or-outer question all three agree, and it is the same split cuBLAS uses: an outer product inside each PE and an inner product across PEs. The first is forced by the instruction — one scalar port and two vector ports leave no way to issue a dot product — so every scheme picks a scalar to broadcast and a vector to stream. Where they part ways is the sum over k: MeshGEMM performs it over time on one PE, so no partial sum ever crosses the fabric, while MeshGEMV and the Hot Chips design spread K across PEs and pay for a reduction. Each half of that row opens the matching view.

    They are three answers to one problem. Keeping the output still (MeshGEMM) needs no reduction but moves both inputs every step. Keeping the weights still (MeshGEMV) moves nothing until the end, then pays for a reduction and a broadcast. Keeping the activations still (Hot Chips) lets weights flow through without ever being stored, which is what lets one wafer run models far larger than its memory — at the price of a reduction on every output.

    Two corrections to earlier pages in this study

    "These kernels do not multicast" was wrong for MeshGEMV. That conclusion came from auditing packet identities, which can only see a packet split into a tree. A router can also multicast along a line — each PE delivering the packet and forwarding it — and such a packet keeps one identity and a simple path. The compiled route tables show it directly: MeshGEMV's colour 9 delivers and forwards at its middle PEs, and each of its 352 packets is seen at all four rows. MeshGEMM's kernel colours never do; its only deliver-and-forward routes belong to the memcpy runtime.

    Low arithmetic intensity is not what limits one PE. An earlier comparison with GPU register tiling called it the ceiling behind 3.20 multiply-accumulates per cycle. Hot Chips 2022 documents a memory that delivers two full reads and one full write every cycle — exactly one memory-to-memory FMA — plus a software cache for accumulators. The design does not need register reuse. The measured limiter is instruction issue: FMAs go out every 9 cycles while occupying the lanes for 4.
